## Delivered and accepted

Delivered in v1.1.1, published 3 October 2026. Jon confirmed this outcome and
its three success criteria as delivered and accepted on 11 October 2026. This is
a retrospective record written on that date; it does not imply the outcome ID
or this wording existed at the time of delivery.

## Why this mattered

A household should not have to repeat installation work each time the product
improves. It should choose whether updates happen automatically, retain its wishes
and family setup, and have a clear recovery path if an update fails.

## What the history establishes

The earlier v1.0.0 release explicitly left genuine scheduled delivery unverified.
The 3 October record closes that particular evidence gap: a separate test household
received an actual scheduled event at 19:11:02 UTC and served the new version at
19:12:03 UTC. Its seeded members, lists, wishes, claims and migration records were
unchanged. A separate failed-build exercise retained the previous version and data;
retry then succeeded. Local integration checks cover preservation of settings and
household workflows and refusal to overwrite conflicting local changes.

Version 1.1.1 was published on 3 October at 21:43 UTC. The live exercise used a
published test release in disposable repositories, not a claim that every real
household automatically received the project release. Its test version v1.1.2 is
not a fourth release of this product. The pinned readiness record links the
underlying test runs and distinguishes the candidate and fixture revisions.

## Boundary

Manual updating remains the default; automatic delivery requires opt-in. The first
observed scheduled event took about three hours and fourteen minutes to arrive.
There is no promise of immediate or punctual updates.

The live test used synthetic household data and did not repeat authenticated
family journeys or complete a fresh-account installation. Retaining the prior site
was observed for the tested failed build; this is not a general guarantee of
rollback after every possible update failure. No maintenance-time saving or
adoption benefit has been measured. No original target date has been invented.
